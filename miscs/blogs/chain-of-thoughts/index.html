Research Chain-of-Thoughts

Updated: October 29, 2026

Many students, including me when I was a student, struggle to arrange their thoughts. The consequences for this is that we cannot make presentations that engage the audience, we cannot highlight the importance of our research, and we cannot showcase our caliber as a researcher. I have spent some time to think about this and formed a research chain-of-thoughts, which can be helpful in presentation delivery and manuscipt writing, where storytelling is important, and in research, where critical thinking and logical flow is the key. So here is the chain-of-thought I am talking about.

  • Grab attention: The first 30-60 seconds of a talk is the most important, because it will determine whether people are interested in you or your research, and whether they will continue to focus to the rest of your talk. This is especially important nowadays when people’s attention span has become shorter and shorter. Try to start with something simple, relatable, or intriguing. I usually use a simple, specific, relatable, and preferably funny example that motivate my research.

  • Story unfolding: After grabbing people’s attention, your story starts. And to tell a story, you have to have events that fold and unfold. In research, that’s when you talk about the questions you want to answer, the method that people have started with, the challenges with this method, what people does next, etc. One thing motivates the next.

  • Climax: Show how your method can overcome all the problems that you have presented. This can be done by comparing your methods to existing methods in both existing and new tasks, and show how your method perform or bring new insights.

  • Contemplation: This is where your critical thinking comes in. Is your method generalizable enough to consider more complicated situations? Anticipate where your method can go wrong. What do you do to safeguard it? This can comes from sensitivity analysis, external validation, etc. This part should showcase your research competency.

  • Finally: The story ends and you should make people feel excited for the future of this research. Address where this will go, what else can be done, and what applications can this enable.
